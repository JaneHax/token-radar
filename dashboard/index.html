The multiple is measured, then tested on data it never saw

The snapshot records both TVL and market cap for protocols whose token already trades. Fitting those two against each other gives the model the board uses:

It is a curve, not a constant, because a protocol's market-cap-to-TVL multiple is not constant: it falls as a protocol gets bigger. The bands below are the raw evidence, straight from the snapshot, and the whole reason a single flat multiple loses.

What it costs in accuracy

A model that will not state its error beside its rivals' is a claim, not a model. Both tests hold out data the fit never saw. Lower is better.

Model Leave-one-out Walk-forward

The error is the median symmetric MAPE, so 105% means the typical held-out miss is about a factor of two in either direction. That is large, and it is stated because it is true: a protocol's launch value is not knowable to better than that from TVL alone. The board ranks by it anyway, and the ranking is the model's claim rather than a price.

The category medians the first version of this radar used lost to the power law on both tests and are gone. What survives of category is the structural filter that decides which protocols are candidates at all, not what they are worth.

How a row is scored

Every pre-token protocol in the snapshot is scored by one function. No name is special-cased, and no constant is typed in by hand: the curve is fitted on the snapshot at run time. 

fit     = least squares of log(mcap) on log(tvl), over every trading protocol
base    = fit.a × tvl_usdfit.b
growth  = clamp(change_7d, −50, +100) ÷ 100 × 0.6
tenure  = 0.75 + min(age_days, 1095) ÷ 1095 × 0.50
reach   = 1 + min(chains − 1, 12) × 0.02

implied = base × (1 + growth) × tenure × reach
fit
The power law between TVL and market cap, measured on the protocols that already trade. Its exponent is below 1, which is the whole finding: bigger protocols trade at smaller multiples. A flat multiple throws that away, and loses on both held-out tests.
growth
Seven-day TVL change, clamped to −50%..+100% and weighted 0.6. A protocol losing TVL is a launch losing its reason; one adding it is being used right now.
tenure
How long it has run with no token. A protocol four years in has built usage a launch would finally price; a two-week-old one has a story, not a business. Ramps 0.75 to 1.25 over three years; an unrecorded age takes 0.95.
reach
Chains it is deployed on. Fourteen chains is fourteen communities reachable at launch. +2% a chain, capped at twelve.

Which protocols are candidates at all

Pre-token means no market cap, no CoinGecko id and no symbol. On top of that the board applies the snapshot's own structural rule below its size floor: not a bridge, canonical bridge, liquid staking, restaking, restaked BTC or CEX; no version marker at the end of the name; no whole word wrapped, staked or staking; and not an exchange's own product. WBTC is Bitcoin on Ethereum and Binance's staked ETH is a staking receipt, so neither is a protocol waiting on a launch, at any size. Ranking them would put products at the top of a board built to find protocols with no token yet.

What the board is not

The implied value is the model's claim about what a launch could be worth, not a forecast of a price and not advice. It is off by about a factor of two on held-out data, and the Model tab says so with the numbers. A protocol at the bottom of the board is no worse a subject than one at the top: it is smaller, or shrinking, or new, and its plan says which.
