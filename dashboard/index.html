The multiple is measured, not assumed

The snapshot records both TVL and market cap for protocols whose token already trades. For each category, the model takes the median of market cap / TVL across those protocols and uses it as the multiple a launch in that category would carry. A category needs three trading peers to calibrate on its own; thinner ones fall back to the median across all trading protocols, ×.

Nothing here is a hardcoded list of names. Feed the model a different snapshot and it recalibrates from that snapshot's own market.

How a row is scored

Every pre-token protocol in the snapshot is scored by one function. No name is special-cased.

base    = tvl_usd × categoryMultiple(category)
growth  = clamp(change_7d, −50, +100) ÷ 100 × 0.6
tenure  = 0.75 + min(age_days, 1095) ÷ 1095 × 0.50
reach   = 1 + min(chains − 1, 12) × 0.02

implied = base × (1 + growth) × tenure × reach
categoryMultiple
The median market-cap-to-TVL multiple the snapshot's own trading protocols in that category carry. This is what a launch in that category is worth per dollar of TVL, measured.
growth
Seven-day TVL change, clamped to −50%..+100% and weighted 0.6. A protocol losing TVL is a launch losing its reason; one adding it is being used right now.
tenure
How long it has run with no token. A protocol four years in has built usage a launch would finally price; a two-week-old one has a story, not a business. Ramps 0.75 to 1.25 over three years; an unrecorded age takes 0.95.
reach
Chains it is deployed on. Fourteen chains is fourteen communities reachable at launch. +2% a chain, capped at twelve.

What the board is not

The implied value is the model's claim about what a launch could be worth, not a forecast of a price and not advice. A protocol at the bottom of the board is no worse a subject than one at the top: it is smaller, or shrinking, or new, and the plan for it says so.
